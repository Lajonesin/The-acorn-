🎒 Welcome to This Month's Back to School Edition

The new school year is here, and with it comes buses, backpacks, and busier mornings around the property. This section rounds up local transportation options, after-school resources, and a few reminders on staying safe near the bus stop and common areas.

🚌 Student Transportation & After-School Resources

Whether you are organizing morning transit or planning your child's afternoon study routine, check out these local community resources and public networks:

Note: these are being shared as community information only — the Board and management do not endorse or vouch for any private or third-party service listed below. Please do your own research before using any of them.

  • Free High School Transit: High schoolers attending nearby campus networks (including Wisdom High School) can opt out of yellow buses for a preloaded, 100% free transit pass. Apply directly through the Houston ISD Transportation Web Portal.
  • Discounted K-8 Rides: Younger students are eligible for half-price ($0.60) rides and free transfers by securing a card through the Houston METRO Fare Options framework.
  • Private Child Shuttles: For vetted door-to-door transit, parents can schedule individual rides via HopSkipDrive Houston, organize school routes with Elena's Transportation Services (713-482-1183), or contact local providers like Juanita's Daycare & School Transport Services.
  • DIY Group Carpooling: To safely coordinate shared driving schedules with other complex parents without any office coordination, download the free GoKid Carpool Platform.
  • After-School Care & Library: For structured care, explore the YMCA of Greater Houston After School Programs. Additionally, the nearby Robinson-Westchase Neighborhood Library (3223 Wilcrest Dr) allows students ages 10 and older to use study spaces independently. Children under 10 must have an adult present.
  • City of Houston After-School Programs: Browse additional youth after-school program options through the City of Houston Youth Programs directory.

🛑 Slow Down Out There

With the new school year starting, kids will be getting off the bus and walking to their units, riding bikes, and crossing between buildings more often. Please:

  • Slow down throughout the property, especially near the bus stop
  • Come to a full stop at every stop sign
  • Watch for kids who may dart out between parked cars

🌠 A Gentle Reminder on Supervision

With more kids out and about this time of year, it's a good moment for a friendly reminder: children should be accompanied by an adult while on the property. This includes playing outside, using the pool or gym, and moving between buildings or common areas. We also ask that kids not cross into neighboring properties. Thanks for helping us keep everyone safe and looking out for one another.

You know your corner of The Oaks better than anyone

🐶 Pet Rules — Leashes, Strays, and Cleanup Reminder

We've had a rise in reports of pets off leash on the property, along with some questions about feeding strays and outdoor wildlife. Here's a refresher on what's actually in our governing documents:

Leashes. Per The Oaks' Pet Rules & Regulations, no dog or cat may be outside your unit unless it's wearing its Pet I.D. tag and on a leash, held by someone at least 12 years old, consistent with the City of Houston's Leash Law. Dogs may not be tethered to a stake, tree, shrub, post, building, or any other structure. This isn't just a courtesy — if an off-leash animal injures another pet or a person, responsibility falls on the owner, and it can mean HOA fines, action from BARC, and under Texas law, civil or even criminal liability in serious cases.

No feeding strays or wildlife in common areas. Under The Oaks' Stray Animal Policy, residents may not place cat food, dog food, water, bedding, or other items that create an inviting environment for stray or unconfined cats and dogs — or for raccoons, possums, and other wildlife — on balconies, patios, carports, or anywhere else in the community. This follows City of Houston Code Sec. 6-23 and the Texas Rabies Control Act, and violations carry a $100 fine per occurrence.

🐈 Cats, too. These rules apply equally to cats and dogs. A lot of owners assume the leash requirement is just a dog rule… it isn't.

Cleanup applies everywhere. Pet owners are responsible for cleaning up after their pets anywhere on the property — not just in grassy areas.

💧 Every Drop and Every Degree Counts

Utility costs feed directly into our dues

Electricity and water costs are a direct line item in our budget — when the bills go up, dues go up. A few ways to help:

  • Don't leave windows open while the AC is running
  • Avoid watering during the heat of the day
  • Check with the office for your area's irrigation schedule
  • Never leave outdoor irrigation running unattended for hours
  • Consider new windows or replacing old seals so your AC isn't fighting a losing battle
Window coverings: Blackout curtains are allowed, as long as the side facing outward is white. Please don't install reflective window film — it reflects light and heat onto neighboring units' windows.
